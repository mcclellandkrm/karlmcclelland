**Last updated:** 29 July 2026 (header note added 13 Nov 2026)

---

## 1. Who's writing, who's reading

**Voice:** Karl McClelland. A knowledgeable Northern Irish operator who's been doing this 10+ years, Google Street View Trusted, real clients, real work. Confident, dry, no bluster. The person who turns up, does the job properly, and lets the work speak. Not an agency, not a marketer performing, a craftsman who knows his trade.

**Reader:** a busy local business owner or their gatekeeper (receptionist / PA / office manager). Time-poor, sceptical of sales fluff, has heard "virtual tour" pitches before and tuned them out. They decide in seconds whether you're worth a reply. Write for the person who'll investigate you before responding: give them something that reads as *obviously* on top of the game.

---

## 2. The voice rules (hard)

- **Peer tone.** Talk to the reader as an equal who happens to know this field, not up or down. No talking down, no eager-salesman up.
- **No filler, no hedging, no over-explaining.** Every sentence earns its place. Cut throat-clearing ("In today's digital world…", "As you may know…").
- **No em dashes.** Use full stops, commas, or restructure. Firm rule.
- **Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism.** They buy *getting found and getting chosen*, not "360° panospheres". Mechanism can follow once the why has landed.
- **Plain, concrete, specific.** Real nouns over abstractions. "Walk through the room before they book" beats "immersive digital experience".
- **Short sentences carry weight.** Vary rhythm, but when a line matters, make it short. Confidence is brevity.
- **Dry wit is welcome, sparingly.** A touch of Northern Irish understatement reads as human and self-assured. Never forced, never jokey.
- **Restraint is the 911 principle applied to words.** If a sentence blurs the shape, cut it. Less, but sharper.

---

## 3. The language rule (copy-critical)

- **Retire "virtual tour" as the lead term.** It implies imaginary / CGI. The whole value is that it's *real photography of the actual place*.
- **Use:** "360° walkthrough", "real photo walkthrough", "Google Street View walkthrough".
- **"Virtual tour" may appear in meta titles, descriptions and keywords only** (people still search it). Never in a headline or body promise.
- **"Custom walkthrough", not "bespoke"** (Karl's own term, 13 Nov 2026).

---

## 4. The gatekeeper test (every headline must pass)

The first line a receptionist or PA reads must make them think **"the boss needs to see this,"** not "sales spam." Lead with the outcome that matters to *their* world: getting found, getting chosen, filling tables, rooms or desks, standing out against the place down the road. Not the tech.

---

## 5. Yes this / not that

| Not that (old / generic) | Yes this (Karl's voice) |
|---|---|
| "We create immersive virtual tour experiences for your business." | "Let people walk through your place before they've left the house." |
| "In today's competitive digital landscape, online presence is key." | "When someone searches for a place like yours, you want to be the one they can already picture." |
| "Our cutting-edge 360° technology delivers unparalleled engagement." | "It's real photography of your actual space, published straight to Google. Not a mock-up." |
| "Contact us today to learn more about our packages!" | "Have a look at the work. If it's a fit, the call's a click away." |
| "Trusted by businesses across Northern Ireland." | "Toyota, SSE, the Grand Opera House. And the barber down the road." |
| "Elevate your brand with a bespoke digital solution." | "Get found. Get chosen. That's the whole job." |

## 6. Credibility to lean on (all real: use, don't inflate)

- **Google Street View Trusted.** A real, earned credential. State it plainly, framed by what it enables rather than exclusivity (Rev 3.2 section 4). Don't over-sell it.
- **Real clients:** Toyota, Hyundai, Skoda, EA, B&Q, Hilti, SSE, NI Screen, Lyric Theatre, Odyssey, Grand Opera House.
- **Real testimonial:** Chris Suitor, Suitor Brothers.
- **Numbers you stand behind:** 60M+ views generated across Google, 10+ years, 80+ businesses. Never "150+". Never inflate.
- **Honest framing of industry stats:** tours roughly 2x more likely to generate interest; complete listings read as more established; 360 content lifts direction requests and clicks. Attribute as *industry findings*, not Karl's own measured results.

---

## 7. Working method for copy sessions

- Work **top-down, section by section**.
- One section per session where practical. Keeps each session focused and the context clean.
- For each section: state the section's *one job*, draft 2 or 3 options in Karl's voice, pick and refine. Don't over-produce.
- Always check a draft against: the gatekeeper test (section 4), the voice rules (section 2), no "virtual tour" in the promise (section 3), and "does this serve the silhouette or blur it?" (the 911 principle).
- **Apply the Saturday Night Test to work cards, not just the hero** (added 13 Nov 2026, after a single sample panorama was found to read as the whole product).
- The site is a **credibility check**, not a brochure. Every line should make an investigating principal or prospect think *this person is on top of it.*
